📖 Psychological Theory: Carl Jung's Cognitive Types

The 16 cognitive personality archetypes originate from Swiss psychiatrist Carl Gustav Jung's 1921 treatise Psychologische Typen (Psychological Types):

  • Cognitive Functions: Jung posited that conscious experience is mediated through four core mental functions: two perceiving functions (Sensing and Intuition) and two judging functions (Thinking and Feeling), directed either inwardly (Introverted) or outwardly (Extraverted).
  • Constructive Self-Understanding: Identifying your cognitive inclinations assists in recognizing communication mismatches, reducing team friction, and crafting career paths tailored to natural neurological preferences.
  • Independent Educational Purpose: This questionnaire is designed solely for self-exploration and personal development based on publicly discussed typology frameworks.

Theoretical references: Jung C.G. (1921) Psychological Types; Sharp D. (1987) Personality Types: Jung's Model of Typology.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Is this test the official Myers-Briggs Type Indicator (MBTI)?

No. This tool is an independent educational questionnaire based on Carl Jung's Psychological Types theory. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or associated with The Myers & Briggs Foundation.

What are the four primary cognitive preference axes?

The four dichotomies are: Energy Orientation (Introversion vs Extraversion), Information Gathering (Sensing vs Intuition), Decision Making (Thinking vs Feeling), and Lifestyle Structure (Judging vs Perceiving).

Can my personality type change over time?

While baseline cognitive preferences remain relatively stable, adults naturally develop their tertiary and inferior functions through career maturity, relationships, and self-reflection.
